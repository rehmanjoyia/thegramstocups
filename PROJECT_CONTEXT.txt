## 1. Project Overview

This is a specialist cooking measurement website focused initially on:

**Grams to Cups**

The site is being built as a genuinely useful conversion product first, while
also being designed from the beginning for organic search growth.

The objective is NOT to build a generic calculator directory.

The objective is NOT to generate the largest possible number of SEO pages.

The objective is to build a small, focused, fast, accurate, trustworthy
specialist resource that can gradually expand based on demonstrated user and
search demand.

Primary monetization is expected to be Google AdSense after the site has enough
quality content and utility to justify monetization.

## 3. Primary Search Intent

The homepage is the primary authority page for the broad intent:

**grams to cups**

The homepage should also naturally satisfy closely equivalent searches such as:

- grams to cups
- g to cups
- gram to cup
- gram to cups
- convert grams to cups
- grams into cups
- grams to cups conversion
- grams to cups converter
- grams to cups calculator
- convert g to cups

These are variations of substantially the SAME user intent.

They must NOT receive separate landing pages simply because keyword tools report
separate search volumes for them.

---

## 4. Fundamental SEO Rule: One Page Per Intent

We build pages around distinct user/search intents.

We do NOT build pages around every keyword.

Before creating any new indexable URL, ask:

1. Does this represent a meaningfully different user intent?
2. Does it provide standalone utility that the existing page cannot adequately
   provide?
3. Will the page contain materially different information or functionality?
4. Could the query instead be satisfied better by an existing page?
5. Would creating this URL introduce keyword cannibalization?
6. Would the page still be worth publishing if SEO traffic did not exist?

If the page fails these tests, do not create it merely for SEO.

---

## 5. No Synonym Pages

Never create separate pages merely for synonymous formulations such as:

/grams-to-cups/
/convert-grams-to-cups/
/g-to-cups/
/grams-into-cups/
/grams-to-cups-converter/
/grams-to-cups-calculator/

These belong to one primary intent and should consolidate into the homepage.

Do not attempt to "capture more keywords" by creating near-duplicate landing
pages.

---

## 6. Programmatic SEO Rule

Do NOT mass-generate amount-specific pages at launch.

For example, do not automatically create:

/1-grams-to-cups/
/2-grams-to-cups/
/3-grams-to-cups/
...
/1000-grams-to-cups/

Likewise, do not automatically generate every possible combination such as:

/flour/50/
/flour/51/
/flour/52/

The calculator itself should be capable of answering arbitrary amounts without
requiring a separate indexable URL for every input.

Amount-specific pages may be considered later only when there is evidence that
specific pages would provide meaningful standalone value.

Google Search Console data should be an important input into that decision.

Existing competitors ranking with amount pages is evidence that the format can
work. It is NOT automatically evidence that we should reproduce the same
architecture.

## 7. Why Ingredient Pages Are Different

Grams measure mass.

Cups measure volume.

Therefore grams-to-cups conversion depends on the ingredient being measured.

For example:

100 grams of flour

is not the same volume as:

100 grams of honey.

This ingredient dependency is one of the central reasons this website provides
real utility beyond a generic unit converter.

Ingredient-specific pages are therefore legitimate when the conversion,
measurement considerations and supporting information differ materially.

Potential examples include:

- flour
- granulated sugar
- brown sugar
- powdered/icing sugar
- butter
- cocoa powder
- oats
- rice
- honey
- cooking oil
- milk

The final ingredient list should follow the approved research and dataset.

Do not add ingredients solely to increase page count.

---

## 8. Ingredient Pages Must Be Materially Unique

Ingredient pages must NOT be simple keyword-swapped templates.

Changing:

"flour"

to:

"sugar"

while leaving essentially everything else identical is not acceptable.

An ingredient page should contain genuinely relevant information such as:

- ingredient-specific conversion data
- common amounts
- ingredient-specific measurement considerations
- appropriate practical fractions
- applicable density or grams-per-cup assumptions
- aliases where useful
- explanation of variability where relevant
- links to methodology/sources
- relevant related conversions

Shared UI components are encouraged.

Duplicated substantive content is not.

---

## 9. Calculator Accuracy Comes Before SEO

Never change conversion logic simply because a different number looks better for
SEO or matches a competitor.

Conversion accuracy and methodological consistency have priority.

Do not invent ingredient values.

Do not silently copy conversion constants from competing websites.

Do not use arbitrary values generated by an AI model as authoritative ingredient
data.

The calculator must use the project's approved conversion dataset and
methodology.

---

## 10. Conversion Data Model

The project should maintain a structured, centralized ingredient dataset rather
than scattering conversion constants throughout page components.

The exact implementation may evolve, but ingredient records should be capable
of representing information such as:

- canonical ingredient name
- slug
- aliases
- grams-per-cup value and/or appropriate density representation
- applicable cup standard
- source/reference
- measurement assumptions
- rounding behavior where necessary
- explanatory notes
- last-reviewed information if appropriate

There should be one authoritative data source inside the application.

Pages and calculators should consume that source.

Do not duplicate conversion constants in multiple components.

---

## 11. Measurement Transparency

Mass-to-volume conversions for food are often approximate.

Ingredient density can vary because of factors including:

- packing
- moisture
- particle size
- aeration
- product formulation
- ingredient variety
- measurement technique

The website should not present inherently approximate culinary conversions as
laboratory-level exact measurements.

Where relevant, clearly communicate the assumptions used.

The project should define consistently:

- cup standard
- grams-per-cup methodology
- rounding rules
- fraction-display rules
- treatment of ingredient variability

---

## 12. Cup Standard

The project must explicitly choose and document the cup standard(s) used.

Do not silently mix values based on different cup definitions.

If multiple cup standards are eventually supported, they must be clearly labeled
and handled consistently throughout the calculator and dataset.

The final implementation should follow the researched methodology approved by
the project owner.

---

## 13. Result Presentation

The primary calculator should prioritize practical cooking usefulness.

Where appropriate, results may include:

- decimal cups
- practical cooking fraction
- selected ingredient
- amount entered
- measurement assumption/reference
- reverse conversion where useful

Avoid displaying fake precision.

For example, a long decimal such as:

0.832736284 cups

is usually less useful than an appropriately rounded result accompanied by a
practical fraction.

The exact rounding/fraction rules must be consistent throughout the site.

---

## 14. Homepage Is the Most Important Page

The homepage is expected to be the strongest and most important organic landing
page on the site.

Do not fragment its authority across multiple pages targeting the same broad
conversion intent.

The homepage should provide the primary calculator immediately or extremely
close to the top of the page.

Users arriving from Google should not have to scroll through an SEO article
before using the tool.

The homepage should answer:

"I have X grams of ingredient Y. How many cups is that?"

as efficiently as possible.

---

## 15. Homepage Content

Supporting homepage content should exist because it helps users understand or
use the conversion.

Potential sections include:

- primary calculator
- common grams-to-cups conversions
- popular ingredients
- explanation of why ingredient choice matters
- conversion methodology
- common conversion chart
- measurement/cup assumptions
- related tools
- methodology/source link
- genuinely useful questions and answers

Do not add sections merely to increase word count.

There is no target word count that overrides usefulness.

---

## 16. Mobile-First UX

A large percentage of users may be converting measurements while cooking.

Mobile usability is therefore critical.

The main conversion workflow should be exceptionally simple:

1. Enter grams.
2. Select/search ingredient.
3. Receive result immediately.

Avoid unnecessary modal dialogs, page reloads or complicated multi-step forms.

Important controls should be touch-friendly.

The calculator should remain easy to use on a phone in a kitchen environment.

---

## 17. Speed and Technical Simplicity

Performance is a product feature and an SEO requirement.

Prefer:

- lightweight pages
- minimal unnecessary JavaScript
- minimal dependencies
- optimized fonts/assets
- optimized images
- stable layouts
- server-rendered or statically rendered SEO content where appropriate
- fast calculator interactions

Do not introduce a large library or framework dependency for functionality that
can be implemented simply.

Do not sacrifice maintainability merely to achieve tiny theoretical performance
gains, but challenge unnecessary technical complexity.

---

## 18. Crawlability

Important SEO content must be accessible to search engines.

Do not make critical page content available only after complicated client-side
interaction.

Interactive calculator functionality can use JavaScript, but the page should
still expose meaningful crawlable content and metadata.

Important internal links should use normal crawlable links.

---

## 19. Initial Supporting Tool Direction

A reverse conversion such as:

**Cups to Grams**

represents a legitimately different conversion direction and may have its own
dedicated page/tool.

Other adjacent tools should NOT automatically be created at launch.

Possible future expansions might include cooking-related conversions such as:

- grams to tablespoons
- grams to teaspoons
- grams to milliliters
- ounces to cups
- cups to ounces
- milliliters to cups

However, these should be added only after evaluating:

- search intent
- topical fit
- SERP opportunity
- usefulness
- potential dilution of the site's focus
- Search Console/user evidence

Do not turn this project into a generic unit-conversion website without explicit
approval.

---

## 20. Internal Linking

Internal linking should reinforce the site's topical structure.

The homepage should link naturally to important ingredient pages and major
related tools.

Ingredient pages should link back to the main calculator and to closely related
resources where genuinely useful.

Related links should be contextually relevant.

Do not create giant keyword-stuffed link blocks solely for SEO.

Avoid orphaned indexable pages.

---

## 21. URL Architecture

URLs should be:

- short
- human-readable
- stable
- lowercase
- descriptive
- free from unnecessary parameters for canonical content

Avoid unnecessary nesting.

Do not change established URLs casually after launch.

If URLs must change, implement appropriate redirects and update internal links,
canonicals and sitemap entries.

---

## 22. Canonicalization

Every indexable canonical page should have the correct canonical URL.

Do not canonicalize genuinely distinct ingredient pages back to the homepage.

Do not allow parameterized calculator states to accidentally create large
numbers of indexable duplicate URLs.

If query parameters are used to represent calculator inputs, treat their SEO
behavior intentionally.

---

## 23. Sitemap and Robots

Maintain a clean XML sitemap containing canonical indexable pages.

Do not put junk, parameter variations or intentionally non-indexable URLs in the
sitemap.

robots.txt should be intentional and minimal.

Do not use robots.txt as a substitute for proper indexation/canonical strategy.

---

## 24. Structured Data

Use structured data only when it accurately represents visible page content and
is appropriate under current search-engine documentation.

Potentially appropriate structured data may include things such as:

- Organization/WebSite where justified
- BreadcrumbList on relevant internal pages

Other schema should be evaluated against current Google documentation before
implementation.

Do NOT:

- fabricate reviews
- fabricate ratings
- add fake AggregateRating
- mark invisible content as structured data
- use irrelevant schema simply to obtain rich results
- assume FAQ markup will produce rich results

Structured data should describe the page, not manipulate the SERP.

---

## 25. Metadata

Every important indexable page should have intentional:

- title
- meta description
- canonical
- H1
- social/Open Graph metadata where appropriate

Titles should be written for humans while clearly communicating the search
intent.

Do not keyword-stuff titles.

Do not create multiple pages merely to place different keyword variations in
title tags.

---

## 26. Heading Structure

Use semantic headings.

Generally, one clear primary H1 should describe the page.

H2/H3 sections should reflect actual content hierarchy rather than being used as
keyword containers.

Do not force exact-match keywords into every heading.

---

## 27. Content Quality

Content exists to improve the tool experience and answer genuine questions.

Avoid:

- generic AI filler
- repetitive introductions
- keyword stuffing
- unnecessarily long explanations
- fabricated facts
- paragraphs that merely restate the calculator result
- identical FAQ blocks across many pages
- template text whose only variation is an ingredient name

Short useful content is better than long useless content.

---

## 28. Research and Sources

Maintain transparency about conversion methodology.

Where appropriate, provide users with methodology/reference information.

Research sources should be recorded so important conversion constants can be
audited later.

Competitor values should not automatically be treated as authoritative sources.

If reputable sources disagree, document the discrepancy and make an intentional
decision rather than silently choosing whichever value is convenient.

---

## 29. Competitor Benchmark

gramstocup.com is an important competitive benchmark because previous research
found that it achieved substantial estimated organic visibility despite
relatively low apparent domain authority and a small backlink profile.

The strategic lesson is NOT:

"Clone gramstocup.com."

The strategic lesson is:

"Google has demonstrated willingness to rank tightly focused specialist sites
for this topic."

Use competitor research to understand:

- search intent
- useful page types
- SERP behavior
- content gaps
- UX weaknesses
- expansion opportunities

Do not copy competitors' text, design, data or site structure blindly.

Our goal is to build a better product.

---

## 30. EMD Strategy

The production domain is expected to be an exact-match or closely keyword-rich
domain.

Treat this primarily as:

- clear topical branding
- memorability
- relevance communication

Do not assume the EMD guarantees rankings.

Do not compensate for an EMD with excessive exact-match optimization.

The site should look and behave like a legitimate specialist product/brand, not
a disposable SEO microsite.

---

## 31. AdSense Strategy

AdSense monetization is secondary to the primary user task.

The calculator must remain easy to use after ads are introduced.

Ads must not:

- obstruct calculator controls
- masquerade as navigation or conversion results
- create excessive layout shift
- overwhelm the above-the-fold experience
- make the website appear built primarily to display ads

Where ad positions are planned before monetization, reserve space appropriately
to reduce layout shift.

Do not weaken the product simply to increase ad impressions.

---

- About
- Contact

- Disclaimer

- methodology/source information

These pages should reflect the actual site rather than being meaningless
boilerplate where possible.

## 33. Accessibility

Accessibility is part of product quality.

Use:

- semantic HTML
- proper form labels
- keyboard-accessible controls
- sufficient contrast
- understandable validation/error messages
- appropriate focus states
- accessible interactive components

Do not rely solely on color to communicate important information.

## 34. Core Web Vitals

Protect Core Web Vitals throughout development.

Particular attention should be paid to:

- LCP
- INP
- CLS

Do not wait until the entire site is finished to think about performance.

Avoid introducing layout shifts through calculator results, ads, images or
dynamically injected UI.

## 35. Search Console and Analytics

Post-launch decisions should increasingly be based on real data.

The site should be prepared for:

- Google Search Console
- appropriate analytics
- indexation monitoring
- query monitoring
- landing-page monitoring
- Core Web Vitals monitoring

Do not create new SEO pages solely because an SEO tool suggests thousands of
keyword combinations.

Search Console impressions and user behavior should help guide expansion.

---

## 36. Post-Launch Expansion Philosophy

Launch focused.

Observe.

Improve.

Then expand.

Potential expansion should be based on evidence such as:

- Search Console queries
- impressions
- ranking positions
- user behavior
- internal searches
- ingredient demand
- conversion-tool usage
- SERP changes

If the homepage begins receiving impressions for a specific query, first ask
whether the homepage or an existing ingredient page can satisfy that query
better.

Do not automatically create a new URL.

---

## 37. Amount-Specific Pages After Launch

Amount pages are not permanently forbidden.

They are simply NOT the default architecture.

Before creating something such as:

/100-grams-to-cups/

evaluate whether:

- meaningful search demand exists
- the current homepage already satisfies the intent
- the SERP favors dedicated amount pages
- a dedicated page can provide substantially more value
- creating it could cannibalize the homepage
- similar pages would create a thin-content pattern

Test deliberately rather than generating hundreds at once.

---

## 38. AI Overview / Zero-Click Risk

Search engines may answer simple conversions directly or through AI-generated
results.

Therefore the site's value cannot depend entirely on displaying one static
number.

Our product should provide additional utility through areas such as:

- arbitrary amount conversion
- ingredient-specific calculation
- ingredient search
- practical cooking fractions
- reverse conversion
- common conversion tables
- measurement context
- transparent methodology

Give users a reason to use the tool rather than merely reading a one-line search
answer.

---

## 39. Avoid Unnecessary Feature Creep

Do not add features merely because they are technically interesting.

Every substantial feature should support at least one of:

- conversion usefulness
- accuracy
- user experience
- accessibility
- trust
- SEO/search intent
- maintainability
- measured user demand

Challenge unnecessary complexity before implementing it.

---

## 40. Agent Behavior

Before making a significant change, an AI agent should:

1. Read this file.
2. Inspect the existing implementation.
3. Review relevant research documentation when the decision depends on research.
4. Identify whether the proposed change conflicts with an established project
   decision.
5. Ask the owner about genuinely unresolved strategic decisions rather than
   silently inventing them.
6. Implement only after the intended direction is clear.
7. Test the change.
8. Preserve existing working functionality unless the change intentionally
   replaces it.
9. Update documentation when an approved architectural or strategic decision
   changes.

---

## 41. Do Not Silently Make SEO Strategy Decisions

An implementation agent should not independently decide to:

- generate hundreds of pages
- change URL architecture
- target a new keyword category
- create synonym landing pages
- index calculator parameter URLs
- remove existing indexed pages
- change canonical strategy
- change the conversion methodology
- substantially expand the site's topical scope

without discussing the decision with the owner.

Implementation convenience must not dictate SEO architecture.

---

Multiple AI agents may work on this project.

Do not allow different agents to independently redesign the project based on
their preferences.

This file and approved owner decisions take precedence over an agent's preferred
architecture.

A secondary agent may audit or criticize an implementation.

Its findings should be evaluated and then incorporated deliberately rather than
allowing multiple agents to perform competing rewrites.

There should remain one coherent implementation.

## 45. Final Principle

When uncertain, prefer:

**fewer excellent pages over many weak pages**

**one authoritative page over several cannibalizing pages**

**accurate calculations over convenient numbers**

**real utility over SEO filler**

**simple architecture over unnecessary complexity**

**measured expansion over speculative programmatic SEO**

The goal is not to look like the largest grams-to-cups website on launch day.

The goal is to become the most useful and trustworthy specialist result for the
problem we are targeting.
